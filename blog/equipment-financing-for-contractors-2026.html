Equipment Financing for Contractors: How It Works and Who Qualifies

October 20, 2026  ·  JWAT Enterprises Inc
← Back to Blog

Equipment financing lets contractors borrow money specifically to buy or lease tools, vehicles, or machinery — using the equipment itself as collateral. If you're tired of turning down jobs because you don't have the right equipment, or you're draining working capital on purchases that should be financed, this guide walks you through exactly how equipment financing works and what lenders actually look at.

What Equipment Financing Actually Is

Equipment financing is a loan or lease tied directly to a piece of equipment. The equipment acts as collateral, which means lenders take on less risk — and that usually makes approval easier compared to an unsecured business loan.

When the loan is paid off, you own the equipment outright. With a lease, you make payments for a set term and then either return it, renew, or buy it at a residual value.

For contractors, this matters because:

  • You preserve cash for payroll, materials, and overhead
  • You can take on larger jobs without waiting to save up
  • Payments are often structured to match the revenue the equipment generates
  • Interest and depreciation may be tax-deductible (check with your tax professional)

What Types of Equipment Can Be Financed?

Most revenue-generating equipment qualifies. For contractors, that typically includes:

  • Excavators, backhoes, and bulldozers
  • Dump trucks, flatbeds, and work vans
  • Cranes and lifts
  • Generators and compressors
  • HVAC systems and plumbing tools
  • Trailers and skid steers
  • Welding equipment and forklifts

Both new and used equipment can often be financed. Used equipment loans may come with slightly different terms depending on the age and condition of the asset.

Who Qualifies for Equipment Financing?

Qualification requirements vary by lender, but marketplace lenders generally look at a broader picture than a traditional bank. Here's what they typically evaluate:

Factor What Lenders Look At
Credit Score 500+ is often considered. Higher scores unlock better rates.
Time in Business Most lenders prefer 6 months or more in operation.
Monthly Revenue Consistent deposits signal repayment ability. Minimums vary by lender.
Equipment Type Revenue-generating assets with resale value are easiest to finance.
Bank Statements Lenders typically want 3 months of business bank statements.

If a bank has already declined your application, that doesn't automatically disqualify you with a marketplace lender. The collateral nature of equipment financing, combined with your revenue history, often carries more weight than your credit score alone.

Equipment Financing vs. Other Funding Options

Not every funding tool is built for equipment. Here's how the main options stack up for contractors:

Option Best For Typical Drawback
Equipment Loan Buying equipment you'll own long-term Equipment is collateral — lender can repossess if you default
Equipment Lease Staying current with newer models You don't own it at the end unless you buy it out
Working Capital Loan Cash flow gaps, payroll, materials Not equipment-specific; may carry higher rates
Bank SBA Loan Large purchases with strong credit history Slow approval, strict requirements, months of paperwork
Revenue-Based Financing Flexible repayment tied to cash flow Repayment scales with revenue — good and bad

Many contractors combine options — using an equipment loan for a truck and a working capital line for materials on the same project. Marketplace lenders can often help you access both through a single application.

Common Mistakes Contractors Make When Financing Equipment

Getting approved is only half the job. Here's what to watch for:

  • Underestimating total cost of ownership. Factor in insurance, maintenance, and fuel before committing to a monthly payment.
  • Financing equipment you don't need yet. If the job isn't confirmed, hold off. Monthly payments start regardless of your revenue.
  • Ignoring the soft-pull option. Some contractors apply to multiple lenders separately, each triggering a hard pull. Use a marketplace that starts with a soft pull to protect your score.
  • Mixing equipment financing with personal credit. Keep business financing in your business name. It protects your personal credit and helps build your business credit profile over time.
  • Waiting until you're desperate. Applying after a cash crisis puts you in a weaker position. The best time to explore financing is before you urgently need it.

How to Strengthen Your Application Before You Apply

A few simple steps can improve your chances and the terms you receive:

  1. Check your business credit first. Know what lenders are seeing before they see it. Free monitoring tools let you review your profile and catch errors early.
  2. Keep three months of clean bank statements. Consistent deposits and a positive average daily balance work in your favor.
  3. Have the equipment details ready. Make, model, year, condition, and price — lenders move faster when you come prepared.
  4. Separate your business and personal finances. If you're still running business revenue through a personal account, open a dedicated business checking account before you apply.

When to Use Equipment Financing

Equipment financing makes the most sense when:

  • The equipment directly generates revenue (so payments pay for themselves)
  • Buying outright would drain your working capital below a safe level
  • You have a confirmed job or contract that requires the equipment
  • You want to grow your capacity without waiting 12–18 months to save up

It's worth pausing if the equipment is a "nice to have," the revenue it will generate is uncertain, or your cash flow is already under serious stress.
